# Sunday, July 19 — What Jeremiah 29:11 Actually Means

## The Personal Version

I used to have Jeremiah 29:11 on a coffee mug.

"For I know the plans I have for you," declares the Lord, "plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future."

I thought it meant God was going to give me the job I wanted, the relationship I wanted, the life I wanted. Like He was some kind of cosmic career counselor.

Then I actually read the chapter.

---

## Context That Changes Everything

Jeremiah wrote this to exiles. People ripped from their homes, marched to Babylon, living in a city that worshipped everything they didn't believe in.

God's "plans to prosper you" wasn't about their comfort. It was about their character. He told them to settle in, build houses, plant gardens, seek the peace of the city they were captive in. For seventy years.

Seventy years.

The "hope and future" wasn't an immediate rescue. It was a promise that God was still working while they waited. That their faithfulness in captivity mattered. That He hadn't forgotten them even when everything looked like failure.

---

## My Observation

I've been treating this verse like a promise that my plans will work out. But it's actually a promise that God's plans are bigger than my plans. That He's working when I can't see it. That the waiting isn't wasted.

---

## Application

Where am I trying to rush God's timeline? Where am I treating my discomfort as a sign that something's wrong, instead of an invitation to trust?

---

## Prayer

God, I want the coffee mug version of this verse. The one where everything works out how I want it to. But today I'm choosing to trust that Your plans are better than mine — even when they look like Babylon. Give me faith for the waiting.

---
